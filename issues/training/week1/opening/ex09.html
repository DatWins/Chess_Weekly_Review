3rd 3-0 Thursday, Oct 2026 · TWIC 1667 · White to move after 10...Nbd7

Nimzo-Indian — White to move after 10...Nbd7

Caruana has a pawn on d4 and knight on d5 in the Nimzo-Indian. Black has doubled the c-pawns on White but maintained good piece activity. What is White's best plan?

A 11. 0-0 — complete development
Castle and complete development, connecting rooks and beginning queenside play.
B 11. Bg5 — pin the knight
Pin Black's knight on f6 to increase pressure on d7 and limit Black's options.
C 11. a3 — kick the bishop
Challenge the bishop on b4 immediately to clarify the structure.
✓ Correct — A is the best plan.
✗ Not quite. The answer is Plan A.

Why Plan A?

11. 0-0! is correct — in the Nimzo-Indian, White's extra pawn center requires rapid completion of development. After 11...Nxd5 12. cxd5 Nf6 13. dxc6 bxc6 White has an extra center pawn and better minor pieces.

The Nimzo-Indian is one of the most complex openings in chess. The key strategic concept is that White's doubled c-pawns are weaker than they look — but the powerful pawn center (after d4, c4, and e4) gives long-term compensation. Caruana understands this better than almost any other player.

Plan B (Bg5) is possible but 0-0 is more urgent — Black threatens ...Nxd5. Plan C (a3) weakens the queenside at the wrong moment.
