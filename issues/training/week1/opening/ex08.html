Four Knights — White to move after 6...Bc5

In the Four Knights, Black has placed the bishop on c5. White has a choice between the quiet positional approach and the aggressive central break. What is White's best plan?

A 7. Nd5 — the aggressive Napoleonic move
Immediately jump to d5, challenging both the knight on f6 and the bishop on c5.
B 7. Bc4 — mirror the bishop setup
Mirror Black's setup with the Italian-style Bc4, preparing d3 and 0-0.
C 7. d3 — solid Italian setup
Solidify with d3 and prepare to castle, reaching a known Italian Game structure.
✓ Correct — A is the best plan.
✗ Not quite. The answer is Plan A.

Why Plan A?

7. Nd5! is the famous Rubinstein Attack — by jumping to d5, White immediately challenges the central structure. After 7...Nxd5 8. exd5 Nd4 9. Nxd4 Bxd4 10. c3 Bb6 11. d4 White has a strong center.

The Nd5 idea in the Four Knights is one of chess history's most instructive moves — it challenges Black to either allow White's knight to dominate d5 or exchange it, after which White gets strong central pawns. In the game, Bortnyk pressed Black hard after this opening choice.

Plan B (Bc4) transposes to the Italian Game — playable but less ambitious. Plan C (d3) is too passive in this active Four Knights position.
