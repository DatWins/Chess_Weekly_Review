Titled Tuesday, Oct 2026 · TWIC 1667 · White to move after 10...0-0

Réti with g3 — White to move after 10...0-0

White has chosen the Réti with g3 and Bg2. Black has a solid d5-c6 pawn structure. How should White challenge the center? The choice here defines the entire middlegame plan.

A 11. b3 — prepare Bb2 pressure on d4
Develop the queen's bishop to b2, increasing pressure on the long diagonal and preparing e4 later.
B 11. cxd5 — trade and play on pawn structure
Exchange on d5 to reach an equal pawn structure with slightly more space on the queenside.
C 11. Nc3 — develop and threaten e4
Complete development and prepare the e4 pawn break to challenge Black's center directly.
✓ Correct — C is the best plan.
✗ Not quite. The answer is Plan C.

Why Plan C?

11. Nc3! is the main line — White develops with purpose, preparing e4 to challenge the center. After 11...dxc4 12. e4 White gains central space and the Bg2 becomes powerful on the long diagonal.

In the Réti, the key strategic idea is to use the bishop on g2 to undermine Black's d5-pawn indirectly while developing all pieces harmoniously. The immediate cxd5 (Plan B) releases tension too early and hands Black a comfortable game.

Plan A (b3) is a good alternative but Nc3 is more forcing. Plan B (cxd5) gives up White's structural advantage for nothing.
