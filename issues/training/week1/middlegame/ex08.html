Position after 23.Re2 — Black to move

Nakamura (Black) has a knight on e5 that dominates the center and a queen on e6. White's position looks solid but there is a concrete way to rip it open immediately. What is the best plan for Black?

A 23...Nd4 — centralize the second knight
Transfer the knight to d4 to gain more central control before advancing pawns.
B 23...Qd7 — prepare ...h5 and kingside expansion
Reposition the queen to support a kingside pawn advance.
C 23...f5! — the decisive pawn break
Strike in the center at once. The f-pawn charges forward, opening lines for Black's pieces.
✓ Correct — C is the best plan.
✗ Not quite. The answer is Plan C.

Why Plan C?

23...f5! is the move that decided the game. After 24. exf5 Rxf5, Black's rook activates dramatically and the knight on e5 will soon hop to d3 or f3. Grischuk's position collapses under the pressure.

The game ended: 38. Bh2 h3! 0-1 — the h-pawn cannot be stopped, and if 39. Bg3 then ...Qf2+ mates. A model demonstration of how to convert central domination into a concrete attack.

Plan A (Nd4) is good but slower — the f5 break is immediately winning. Plan B (Qd7) prepares rather than executes — in this position, speed is everything.
