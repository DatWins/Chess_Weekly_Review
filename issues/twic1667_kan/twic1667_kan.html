Kan Sicilian: Black's View

Six practical games with 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 a6 – the queenside expansion, the ...e5 centre, and when to break with ...d5.

The Kan Sicilian (1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 a6) is one of Black's most flexible answers to 1.e4. The early ...a6 is a waiting move with clear strategic intent: it prevents Nb5, prepares ...b5 queenside expansion, and keeps options open in the centre. After 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Black most commonly plays ...e5 (ECO B28, the O'Kelly variation) – immediately seizing central space and forcing the knight to retreat.

These six games from TWIC 1667 cover the main decision points Black faces:

  • After 5.Nc3 ...Bb4: The pin on the knight leads to rich endgames where Black's queenside expansion (a6, b5, b4, a4) proves decisive.
  • After 5.Nc3 Nb3: White avoids complications; Black must choose between sacrificing the a-pawn for centre control or holding it with accurate play.
  • After 5.Bd3: A quieter White system; Black must play actively with ...Nc5 or the thematic ...d5 break before White fixes the structure.
  • The ...d5 break: When and how to play ...d5 is the most important decision in the whole variation – too early and Black loses control, too late and the position becomes passive.
