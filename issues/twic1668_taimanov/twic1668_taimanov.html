Sicilian Taimanov: The 5…a6 Systems

How Black sidesteps the pure Taimanov and keeps the position complex.

Chess Weekly Review · Nov 2026

The Sicilian Taimanov begins with 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6, and after 5.Nc3, Black has a critical choice. The main line goes 5…Qc7, staking out the queenside immediately. But 5…a6 is a subtler approach — Black prepares …b5 and keeps the queen flexible, steering the game into territory where both sides must think for themselves rather than rely on deep preparation.

The games in TWIC 1668 show this line across multiple events, with GM Maxim Matlakov (2596) the standout performer, winning three games as Black in a single Titled Tuesday session. His smooth handling of the resulting structures — often featuring an early …Nxc6 trade leading to a doubled c-pawn for Black, compensated by the bishop pair and active piece play — is the thread running through this issue.

White has many tries on move 6: the aggressive 6.f4, the positional 6.Be2, the sharp 6.Be3 g4, the quiet 6.Nxc6 bxc6 exchange, and the rare 6.a3. Each leads to its own type of middlegame, but the common theme is that Black's 5…a6 gives the second player just enough extra flexibility to create imbalances.
