TWIC 1667 · White's Nf3/b3/Bb2 System

Seven Key Games: Middlegame Plans

From isolation pawn structures to kingside storms – how the world's best players handle the Nf3/b3/Bb2 setup.

Each week The Week In Chess packages the world's tournament output into a single collection. This selection examines seven games from TWIC 1667 in which White employed the Nf3/b3/Bb2 system – a flexible, semi-hypermodern approach that can lead to quiet positional grind or sharp kingside attack depending on how White steers the game.

White's three-move signature – the knight to f3, pawn to b3, bishop to b2 – creates a battery on the long diagonal that is one of chess's most enduring strategic weapons. The games here illustrate the six main middlegame themes that arise once the setup is complete:

  • The isolated d5 pawn: After cxd5 exd5 or …cxd5, Black is left with a lone pawn White targets with the knight-and-queen blockade.
  • The Nc3-Na4-Nc5 tour: The knight manoeuvre that instals a blockader on c5 and trades off Black's dark-squared bishop.
  • The Qa1 battery: Queen retreating to a1 forms the long-diagonal battery and supports a c-file invasion.
  • The c-file highway: Rooks doubled on the c-file, often invading c7, are a recurring motif.
  • Kingside storm (g4-g5, f4-f5, Rf3-h3): Two distinct attacking patterns – the g4 advance and the Zukertort Rf3-h3 swing – both exploiting the Bb2's diagonal.
  • The a4-a5 clamp: Queenside expansion that fixes Black's pawns before White pushes d4-d5 to open the diagonal.
